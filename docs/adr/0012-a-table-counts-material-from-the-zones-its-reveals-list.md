# A table counts the material at its cap from the zones its reveals list, in a payout that can run in parts

> Amended by `docs/adr/0013-no-merges-a-captured-army-vanishes-and-its-captor-is-promoted.md`: there are no rocks; a promoted queen counts 9 and a stone nothing, and the pot includes a stone's stake.

At a table's cycle cap the stakes are split in proportion to the material each standing army has left (pawn 1, knight 3, bishop 3, rook 5, queen 9), so the contract has to find every piece on the board, and the board is facts resolved lazily (`docs/adr/0002-order-free-lazy-resolution.md`): the zone index holds a bit per touched square but cannot be listed, and nothing runs at the end of a cycle to keep a count. So we decided:

- **At a table, the first touch of a zone lists it** (`_tableZones`, in `UsingGameBoard._touch`), and only under a roster, so the world's reveals never pay for it (they read the roster only on a zone nobody has touched yet). The list's order depends on the order of reveals; its SET does not, and the count is a sum, so nobody's share can.
- **The count is one pass over every square that can hold a piece**: the untouched squares of every seat's footprint, then every touched square of the listed zones, each piece counted for the army it resolves to (a rock counts for nobody). It is kept in storage and run by a permissionless `tally(maxSteps)`, so a count too big for one transaction is done in parts, and the first `claim` finishes whatever is left. It reads the board at the cycle after the last, where every fact is final.
- **A table declares its own reveal gas** (`LOBBY_REVEAL_GAS`), the world's worst reveal plus listing two fresh zones, priced in `GasBudget.test.ts`, because the world's `REVEAL_GAS` had no room for it and a reveal that runs out of gas freezes the army.
- **The pot is every army's reserve, debited once** when the first claim writes the outcome: a captured army's reserve is already its captor's, so every stake is in it once, and each army then claims its rounded-down share once, in ETH through `StakeSale.redeem`, paid to its current owner. With no material left anywhere, the standing kings split it evenly.

We rejected a running count per army written by reveals (a capture is only known when a square is next read, so a reveal cannot know what it took without breaking order independence), a list of every touched square (a fresh storage slot per square on the reveal path, where a zone costs one per sixteen by sixteen), and a scan of a bounding box (unbounded in the cap and the seats).
