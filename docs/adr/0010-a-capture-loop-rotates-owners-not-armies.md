# A capture loop rotates the owners of its armies, folded for all of them at once

A capture loop (`docs/adr/0004-capture-loops-rotate-ownership.md`) merges nothing: every army of the ring survives with its king, its pieces and its own reserve, and only its owner changes, to the owner of the army that took its king. So the army id a piece plays for never changes, and the stake moves without a reserve moving, as for a merge (`docs/adr/0009-merges-resolve-through-captured-into-pointers.md`). Whether a capture was part of a loop is only known from the facts of every king of the ring, so it is read lazily: walking the captures from an army (`UsingGameBoard._walk`) and coming back to it is a loop, and that army is its own. The OWNER is the one thing a loop changes that later loops build on, and an army that survives a loop can be in another, so the army record's `owner` is rewritten: when any square of the loop is folded, every army of it gets its captor's owner and `loopedIn` in one go (`_foldLoop`), so a pending loop always reads its captors' owners from before it (`_ownerOf`). We rejected folding one army at a time, which would need each captor's owner as it was before the loop, after later loops may have overwritten it (a history, not a field), and keeping the owner purely lazy for the same reason.

**Consequences.** The army record's `owner` is no longer always the account that created the army: who acts for an army is `_ownerOf`, through `_playerOf`. Loops are seen up to `MAX_CHAIN` (8) armies, like chains; a longer ring reads as captures all round, which leaves every army of it captured. Folding a loop writes two slots per army inside one reveal, so the worst reveal (a slider 8 merges behind taking the king of a loop of 8) is about 1,040,000 gas, and `revealGas` was raised from 800,000 to 1,200,000 (`contracts/test/js/GasBudget.test.ts` prices it per army).
