# An exit removes only the exiting army's own pieces, every piece no army can play again is a rock, and a fold moves the captured reserve up

> Its merged-army part (rocks, `MAX_CHAIN`) is superseded by `docs/adr/0013-no-merges-a-captured-army-vanishes-and-its-captor-is-promoted.md`: an exited army's pieces leave the board, there are no rocks, and the exit still collects every unfolded victim's stake, in parts, except a stone's. A fold still moves the captured reserve up.

Amends `docs/adr/0009-merges-resolve-through-captured-into-pointers.md`.

A king that lands alone on an exit square has left the world at the close of that cycle (`docs/adr/0006-stake-erc20-reserve-exit-only.md`). Removing every piece of its EMPIRE (its own pieces and those of every army it merged) would make every read of a piece ask "is this piece's root exited?", which is the bounded walk of captured-into pointers that `docs/adr/0009-merges-resolve-through-captured-into-pointers.md` keeps to the mover and its destination only. The path squares, a king's neighbours and the footprint squares read only the KIND (`_rawSquareAt`), and the reveal budget depends on that. So we decided:

- **An exit removes only the pieces whose square-recorded army IS the exited army.** That is one comparison on the label the square already holds, so it costs nothing extra on any read. Pieces of the armies it had merged stay on the board as ROCKS.
- **A rock is a piece no army can ever play again**: its walk of captured-into pointers ends at an exited army, or it is more than `MAX_CHAIN` captures from its root. It stays on its square, blocks paths and footprints like any piece, never moves, may be captured by anyone, and capturing it gives nothing. Because a path or a footprint only needs the kind, nobody has to walk anything to treat a rock as an obstacle; the mover and its destination keep the bounded walk, and a rock is never the mover's own.
- **More than `MAX_CHAIN` captures from its root is a permanent rock rule**, replacing 0009's behaviour where a later touch of the square wrote the army reached down and the next read walked on (which made the answer depend on whether and when a square was touched). The walk now stops at `MAX_CHAIN` hops; a piece it could not resolve within them belongs to nobody, for every read, before and after any touch.
- **A fold moves the captured army's reserve up into its captor's.** When `_foldCapture` writes `capturedInto` (a closed-cycle fact), it also moves the victim's whole reserve into the captor's reserve. Every tree sum is unchanged, so the stake reads the same before and after, whatever the order; and trees flatten as squares are touched. This reverses 0009's "no reserve moves", which rejected a move at fold time only because the stake had to be right before the fold; it still is, through the tree walk.
- **The exit pays the whole merged stake, never a truncated one.** `exit(army)`, in its own transaction (never the reveal budget), debits every reserve of the tree it collects and pays the sum to the army's current owner (`_ownerOf`, `docs/adr/0010-a-capture-loop-rotates-owners-not-armies.md`). It walks the tree of who took whom top-down, unbounded in depth (a victim counts when its king really was taken by the army it is listed under, a closed-cycle fact, and an exited tree never changes), folding each victim's capture before debiting the army above it, so no later fold can move a reserve into an army already collected. The walk is kept in storage, so a tree too large for one transaction is first collected by a permissionless, incremental `collect(army, maxArmies)`; collecting is never a precondition of having exited, only of being paid in one go.

We rejected marking an exited empire's pieces gone on every read (a walk on every path square, which grows the worst reveal past the declared gas, and a reveal that runs out of gas freezes the army), and paying only the root's own reserve (it strands the stakes the king won).
