# An army moves up to three pieces a cycle, simultaneously, unless its table turns it off

An army's move is up to three piece-moves, each by a different piece, in the online and offline worlds and at every table whose creator does not turn it off; where it is off, a move is one piece-move, as before. The piece-moves are simultaneous, like every other army's: each is judged legal against the board at the start of the cycle, with the army's own pieces that move this cycle counted as gone, and the order the player lists them in changes nothing. So a piece may move onto, or slide through, a square its own army's piece leaves, and two pieces may swap; two piece-moves to one square, or two by one piece, are refused. Counting its own movers as gone is for the legality check ONLY: what a square's record says stood there, and who could be captured there, stay the start-of-cycle facts, so whichever reveal touches a square first still cannot change what it says (`docs/adr/0002-order-free-lazy-resolution.md`). Across armies nothing changes (`docs/adr/0003-collision-table-king-survives-a-crowd.md`). A deployment stays the whole move of its cycle.

We did it because one move per army lets a lone attacker chase a king forever (`work/notes/ideas/king-chase-table-modes.md`): the king spends its only move running from the square the attacker lands on. With three piece-moves the king can step away while another of its pieces lands on the square it left, where the attacker arrives; the collision table then kills both, so the chase ends in a trade, which the maintainer accepted. We rejected applying an army's piece-moves in the player's order: it is legal under the commit-reveal rule (the order is the player's own, fixed in the commitment), but "own movers count as gone" matches how resolution already works, cannot make the listed order matter, and is simpler to read and test. ADR-0001's objection to more tempo, that a merged empire would snowball, has no object since `docs/adr/0013` removed merges: one stake still buys one army and its three piece-moves, whichever wallet holds it. We dropped the other candidate, a piece guarding its own king's square, for now.

The choice is one bit of the framework's `gameOptions`, and a SET bit means OFF: the world never opens a roster, so it reads zero, and a table created with no options plays like the world. We chose that over giving the world its own deploy-time flag so that one reading serves both.

Amends `docs/adr/0001-one-move-per-army-merge-on-capture.md` (one move per army stays; a move is now up to three piece-moves) and `docs/adr/0014-a-capture-whose-captor-falls-the-same-cycle-leaves-a-stone.md`: its argument that a captor which moved a piece other than its king "had its king stand still" no longer holds, since the king may move in the same cycle. Whether that king fell is still read one level deep, from the square its last move took it to. Spec: `work/specs/tasked/three-piece-moves.md`.
