# A merge is resolved through per-army "captured into" pointers, and no reserve moves

Capturing a king (`docs/adr/0001-one-move-per-army-merge-on-capture.md`) changes who plays every piece of the captured army and what the captor holds, but nothing is rewritten when it happens. Each army keeps the army that took its king, and a piece's army is followed through those pointers when it is read (`UsingGameBoard._armyOf`). Whether a capture happened is only known once its cycle is over, from the square's facts (`ChessRules.kingCapture`, the king rows of the collision table), so until a later reveal touches that square the pointer is read from the facts, and the touch then copies it onto the army record (`capturedInto`). Both sources give the same answer, which is what keeps lazy resolution order-free (`docs/adr/0002-order-free-lazy-resolution.md`): no read order can change who owns a piece or what an army holds.

**No reserve moves on a capture.** The captured army's reserve stays where it is, and the captor's stake is resolved down the tree of who took whom (`UsingGameBoard._stakeOf`), from a per-army list of kings it may have taken, written at reveal time by the reveal that can see the capture is possible and filtered on read. We rejected moving the reserve when the capture is folded, because the fold happens whenever a later reveal touches the square, maybe never, and the stake must be right before then; and moving it at reveal time is impossible, because no single reveal knows the cycle's arrival count. An army reserve is never withdrawable (`docs/adr/0006-stake-erc20-reserve-exit-only.md`), so leaving a captured one in place leaks nothing. The cost falls on the exit, which must collect the reserves of the whole merged tree rather than one.

**Chains are bounded at 8 merges per piece read** (`MAX_CHAIN`). A reveal resolves the army of two pieces only, the mover and its destination (the path and a king's neighbours are read unresolved: a king on the board is never of a merged army), and each merge followed costs about 20,000 gas when its square was never folded, so the bound is what keeps a reveal inside the declared reveal gas, which this raised from 600,000 to 800,000 (`contracts/test/js/GasBudget.test.ts` prices the worst case per merge). A piece more than 8 merges behind its owner reads as the dead army the walk stopped at, on the board and in a reveal alike, until a reveal touching its square writes that army down and the next read walks on. We rejected an unbounded walk (a long-lived empire would make reveals run out of gas, which is a missed reveal and freezes the army), and compressing the pointers on every read (it does not lower the first walk, which is the worst case).
